# Glossary — *Young Gabriel*

A reference for characters, places, kinds, and terms across the saga. Keep this file open while writing chapters to maintain consistency.

---

## Characters

### Gabriel
The Namer. The First Name. Born under a fig tree to a quiet woman of a hill village. Speaks the true names of things. Walks for twenty-three years before he is given his own name by a sheep. Will become the founder of the order called the Gallants. At the time of this volume's events: aged 0 to 27.

**Physical:** Black hair, fine. Brown skin. Eyes the color the morning light was *before* the morning of his birth — which is to say, a color no one else can describe. Small for his age until thirteen, then tall. Lean. Sandalled. Carries one small wrapped bundle his whole walking life.

**Voice:** Quiet. Never shouts. Never argues. Says short sentences with long weights. Asks more than he answers.

### Eshe ("E-shuh")
The Sheep. Born in a normal lambing in Otto's flock. Named by Gabriel at thirteen months. Lives seventeen years — twice the natural span of a sheep. Walks every road Gabriel walks. The world stops noticing her around her tenth year — the eyes of strangers slide off her. The only being who will ever name Gabriel.

**Physical:** Cream wool, oiled. A small leather collar Otto made on the morning of the First Leaving. Bell removed by Gabriel in the third year ("too loud for the listening"). Walks at Gabriel's left hip.

### She Who / Eshmun
Gabriel's mother. A weaver. A widow before Gabriel's birth — her husband, a stoneworker, died in a rockfall four months before. Bore Gabriel alone under what would become a fig tree. Will see her son three more times in her life: at thirteen, at twenty-two, and at her burial. Was the woman who first taught the village to listen, although she did not know that was what she was teaching.

(Note: "Eshmun" is her birth name and is spoken only in flashback. Gabriel calls her *She Who*. The village calls her *Eshmun*. The narrator alternates as appropriate.)

### Otto
A shepherd in the village. Sold Gabriel's mother a wobbly bowl when she was newly widowed. Will spend sixty-three more years telling, with quiet wonder, the story of how the boy named his sheep. Carved Eshe's leather collar in a single night. Plays no further role in the plot but remains the village's living memory of the First Naming until his death.

### Iossa
The carver. An old man two ridges south of the village. Invented the village's first writing on the inside of an oak panel, beginning with the word *ESHE*. Wrote seventeen names before he died. The panel is now in a museum that no one in the village ever lived to see.

### The Nephil at the River (unnamed)
A giant of the kind that came down from the heights. Nine feet tall. Eighty-four years old at the time of meeting. Told for the first time, by Gabriel, what his kind is called. Walks the river for three days with the boy, then turns north to die among his own. One of nine Nephilim Gabriel will meet across the years of his walking; the only one whose meeting is recorded in this volume.

### Vesta
A demigod. Two hundred and seven years old when Gabriel meets her on a winter plain. Had not been given a name in all her life — was called *bastard* by mortals, and nothing by the gods. Gabriel names her *Vesta*: the one who keeps the small fires. She walks away across the plain to every hearth in the world that has been waiting for her.

**Physical:** Black hair to her hips. Linen of the southern plain. Eats peaches out of season. Smells faintly of woodsmoke. Carries no possessions.

### Hestor
King of the yellow-stone city in the south. Fifty years old. Took the name of a small hearth-god into himself in the eleventh year of his reign, in a private moment, and believed it. Has been hosting (and consuming) the dying god ever since. Released by Gabriel in his thirtieth year of rule. Killed by his cousin two months later. Goes into the ground a man — which is, by the embers' reckoning, a good death.

### Memnon
King of a river-delta kingdom in the south-east. The second false king. Has eaten a river-god. Will not let go willingly. Gabriel names him anyway. Half the court drowns in the river-god's return. This is the most violent thing Gabriel will ever do in this volume.

### Coel
Old woman ruler of the high islands. The third false king (the title transcends gender in the embers' reckoning). Swallowed a wind-spirit at twelve. Asks Gabriel for one season to finish the harvest. Gabriel waits. She dies on the first day of winter, with her name on her lips and the wind-spirit free in the open air for the first time in seventy years.

### Godfrey
The first of the fifteen. Born three years after Gabriel's twenty-third winter. Son of a carpenter and a dreaming mother in a small kingdom by the great middle sea. Brown hair, grey eyes, chip on his front tooth. Takes Gabriel's hand on the step of his mother's house. Will train under Gabriel for nine years.

### Galahad, Garrick, Galen, Gaius, Garan, Geraint, Gawain, Goran, Gilead, Greer, Gunnar, Gellis
The other male Gallants. Names only in Book One. Each will have their own arc in Book Two.

### Gisla
The thirteenth Gallant. A girl. The embers warn Gabriel: *her brothers will not understand; you will not allow her to be made small.*

### Michael
The sixteenth name on the list. The only one whose name does not begin with G. *Not mine,* the embers say. *His own.* Comes at the very end. Goes through a door that has not yet been built. Saved for the final book of the saga.

### Maliphant
The thing that has been hosted by Antrum since before language. The eater of the unsaid. Will return many times in the saga. Bound, not destroyed.

## Kinds (Non-Human Beings)

### Embers
Not strictly fire. The settled afterward of fire. Hold the residue of the First Utterance — the original heat that made the world. Every ember in every hearth is the same ember, split and scattered. They remember every word ever said over a hearth. They are the voice in Gabriel's ear from his ninth month onward. They are not gods. They are not spirits. They are an *attention*.

**Voice:** Lower-case in text. Multiple voices speaking in unison; called "the chorus" when speaking at full strength. Use the second-person familiar — "child."

### Nephil (pl. Nephilim)
A kind that came down from the heights. Loved the women of the plain in the long-ago. Their children were neither one thing nor the other. Nine feet tall. Eyes the color of river-stone. Eighty-year lifespans. Dying out in Gabriel's time. There are four great Nephil kinds; Gabriel will meet six Nephilim across the saga.

### Demigod (half-borne)
A thing made when a great power loves a small power and the love has a child. A citizen of neither country. Almost a god, almost a person. Two-hundred-plus-year lifespans. Forty-six known to Gabriel by the end of his walking. The polite word, coined by Gabriel, is *half-borne*. Most call them *bastard*.

### Spirits of Place
The small named spirits: spirits of the bend, of the ford, of the threshold, of the fig. Each tied to one location. Lifespans tied to the location's existence. Cannot leave. Often very lonely.

### Small Gods (Hearth-Gods)
A specific subclass of spirit-of-place. Tied to hearths. Born of the long warmth of a particular fire. Most have been forgotten. Vesta becomes the keeper of all of them.

### Winds
There are many winds. Most are unnamed. Gabriel names four: *Aram* (the wind of the high pass), and three others later.

### Stones
There are nineteen kinds of stone by the end of Gabriel's walking. Each has its own nature. *Anchor* is the stone that pins a place. *Witness* is the stone that records what happens nearby. *Forgetter* is the stone you put on a grave when the dead does not wish to be remembered.

### The Eater (Maliphant)
The worst thing in this volume. The personification of the unsaid. Made of every silence over every hearth that should have spoken. Eats the air, the light, the sound. Cannot exist as itself — must be hosted. After Antrum's naming, walks naked in the world for one minute and is named before it can eat more than a few square meters.

## Places

### The Village (unnamed)
A hill village two days west of the great middle sea. Sheep country. Has no name. Will not have a name until much later, when the Gallants begin to keep maps. Gabriel will return three times: at thirteen, at twenty-two, and at his mother's burial.

### The Fig Tree
On a ridge above the village. Was a tree of no kind until the morning of Gabriel's birth. Bears fruit twice in the spring after his mother is buried beneath it. Still standing in the events of Book Four.

### The Yellow-Stone City
Hestor's capital. Walled in yellow stone. Forgotten two centuries later. The stone quarried for other walls. No surviving records but the embers' memory.

### The Delta Kingdom
Memnon's. Drowns, largely, in the events of Chapter 32.

### The High Islands
Coel's. Survive. Become, in later centuries, a place of pilgrimage for the windkeepers.

### The Carpenter's House
In a kingdom by the great middle sea. Godfrey's birthplace. The site of the last scene of Book One.

### Antrum
The flat round of grey in the far north. Now a place. Has a name. Has a door. Bound. Will not host another guest.

### The Country of Mirrors
A place that does not exist in the waking world. Gabriel dreams of it once, at twenty. Has no face. Is waiting for him. (Foreshadowing for Book Four.)

## Terms

### True Naming
The act of speaking the actual name of a thing such that the thing agrees. Three conditions:
1. The namer must have *attended* the thing — looked at it long enough to see what it is.
2. The named must be willing, or at minimum unable to resist (the not-yet-named cannot resist; the falsely-named can be forced into truth).
3. The name must be spoken kindly. Cruelty in a naming voids it.

### The Braid
What every word ever spoken over a hearth, when laid against every other word, means. The embers hold the braid. Gabriel was given the braid at nine months and seventeen days. He has held it ever since.

### The First Utterance
The voice, in the very beginning, that said one word that became a world. The original heat. The thing every ember is a fragment of.

### A Pact
A binding promise made aloud, witnessed by either an ember or a named element (a river, a wind, a stone). Cannot be broken without consequence. Gabriel makes three pacts in this volume: to the Nephil (he will tell what they were), to the carpenter's wife (he will bring Godfrey back), and to Antrum (he will return).

### The Walking
What Gabriel does for twenty-three years. Capital W. The journey east, south, and finally north, naming what the embers point him toward.

### The Order of Gallants
The order Gabriel will found. The fifteen Gallants and their squires-of-squires. Their work: to repair, in the world, what the embers will break to send Gabriel north. Founded, properly, in Book Two.

## Style Conventions

- **The embers always speak lower-case.** No capitals at the start of their sentences. They are a chorus.
- **Gabriel does not use contractions in speech** until after Antrum. After Antrum, he uses them rarely. They mark his becoming-a-person.
- **Eshe is referred to as "she," never "it."** She is a person, even by the world that does not quite see her.
- **The Nephil at the river is unnamed** — Gabriel did not give him a name because the Nephil did not ask. Respect this.
- **Years are reckoned by Gabriel's age**, not by calendar. The world has many calendars. We use his.
- **Pact-bindings are marked typographically** by italicized full lines on their own:
  > *I will come back.*
